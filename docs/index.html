MODEL SETUP

The curve gives us clues.

Short yields react strongly to expected Fed policy. Long yields also carry expectations about inflation and growth.

01

Read the Treasury curve

The model uses 3-month, 6-month, 10-year, and 30-year Treasury yields. It compares their shape with the Fed’s current target.

02

Teach it with history

We show the model the yield curve from the previous month, then ask whether the Fed raised rates next. Lagging the yields prevents the future from leaking into the prediction.

03

Calculate the hazard

Under the latest curve, the model estimates a 47.7% conditional chance at each scheduled meeting—assuming the curve stays unchanged and no earlier hike has occurred.

04

Move through each month

“A hike at this meeting” and “the first hike happens this month” are different. Scroll through the months to watch survival shrink.

SEPTEMBER

First stop: 47.7%

Nothing has to happen first, so September’s hazard and first-hike probability are the same.

OCTOBER

47.7% hazard, but 24.9% first-hike chance

October must pass through September’s 52.3% “no hike” door first: 52.3% × 47.7% = 24.9%.

NOVEMBER

No meeting, no routine hike

The official calendar has no November FOMC meeting, so the scheduled-meeting probability is 0%. Emergency action is outside this model.

DECEMBER

13.0% chance of the first hike

December can be first only if September and October both pass without a hike: 52.3% × 52.3% × 47.7% ≈ 13.0%.
